PerfectAmino Coated Tabs Ingredients

A line-by-line look at what's inside BodyHealth PerfectAmino Coated Tablets (BHPACT6001), including active components and excipients.

The full ingredient list is on the BodyHealth website and on the bottle. The short version is below — what's in it and why.

Active Ingredients

What's actually in each five-tablet serving:

  • Leucine — the biggest piece of the formula; the amino acid that tells your muscle to start building protein
  • Valine and isoleucine — the other two branched-chain amino acids, paired with leucine for the recovery work
  • Lysine, threonine — the less-glamorous but essential side of the EAA list; collagen, connective tissue, immune function
  • Phenylalanine, methionine, tryptophan — phenylalanine is the one PKU patients have to avoid; methionine drives methylation chemistry; tryptophan is your serotonin and melatonin building block
  • About 4.75 grams of free-form EAAs total per five-tablet serving
  • Coating: hypromellose plus food-grade glycerin — that's the difference between the coated (BHPACT6001) and uncoated PerfectAmino SKUs

Other Ingredients (Excipients)

The non-active ingredients are short and clean: microcrystalline cellulose (a common tablet binder), and the vegetable-based coating (hypromellose plus food-grade glycerin). No magnesium stearate, no titanium dioxide, no synthetic colorants, no shellac glaze, no gluten. That's a meaningfully shorter inactive-ingredient list than most amino acid supplements.

Allergens and Sensitivities

PerfectAmino Coated Tabs are gluten-free, dairy-free, soy-free, corn-free, and non-GMO. The coating is plant-based; the tablets are vegan-suitable. No added flavor, sweetener, or color in the tablet form — those only appear in the powder. If you're a competitive athlete subject to drug testing, note that PerfectAmino doesn't carry an NSF Certified for Sport mark — the formula itself is clean by composition, but the formal certification isn't on it.

Sourcing and Quality Notes

BodyHealth is a small US company in Clearwater, Florida, founded by Dr. David Minkoff. They make the product through pharmaceutical-grade contract manufacturing, source the amino acids from pharmaceutical-grade suppliers, and test every lot for heavy metals and microbial contamination. They'll share the lab paperwork (certificate of analysis) if you ask — which most supplement companies won't. How Ingredients Compare to Similar Products

Where PerfectAmino Coated Tabs sit compared to alternatives: similar quality to Kion Aminos (also EAA-only, also pharmaceutical-grade, but Kion has NSF Certified for Sport and costs a little more). Different from Thorne Amino Complex, which has added arginine and glutamine. Different from a scoop of whey protein, which gives you four times the gross protein but at much higher caloric cost and slower absorption. Inside the BodyHealth line, the coated tabs (BHPACT6001) are the easier-to-swallow option versus the uncoated PerfectAmino SKU; the formula is identical, you're paying a small premium for the coating.
